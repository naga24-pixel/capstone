# A Guide to Deposit Insurance
Source: DICGC (Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation), a wholly owned subsidiary of RBI
URL: https://www.dicgc.org.in/guide-to-deposit-insurance
Retrieved: 2026-08-20

## What's Covered
The DICGC insures all deposits such as savings, fixed, current, recurring, etc. deposits except the following types of deposits:
- Deposits of foreign Governments
- Deposits of Central/State Governments
- Inter-bank deposits
- Any amount due on account of any deposit received outside India
- Any amount which has been specifically exempted by the corporation with the previous approval of Reserve Bank of India

## What's Not Covered
- Deposits mobilized by Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC)
- Deposits mobilized by Land Development Banks
- Mutual funds
- Stocks and bonds
- Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)
- Cryptocurrencies

## Which banks are insured by the DICGC?
Commercial Banks: All commercial banks including branches of foreign banks functioning in India, local area banks and regional rural banks are insured by the DICGC.

Cooperative Banks: All State, Central and Primary cooperative banks (urban cooperative banks) functioning in States/UTs that have amended the local Cooperative Societies Act empowering RBI to order winding up/supersession are covered. At present all co-operative banks are covered by the DICGC. Primary cooperative societies are NOT insured by the DICGC.

## Maximum deposit insured
Each depositor in a bank is insured up to a maximum of ₹5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakhs) for both principal and interest, held in the same right and same capacity, as on the date of liquidation/cancellation of the bank's licence, or the date the scheme of amalgamation/merger/reconstruction comes into force, or imposition of "All Inclusive Directions" restricting withdrawal.

## Aggregation rules
- Deposits kept in different branches of the same bank are aggregated; the ₹5 lakh cap applies to the aggregate.
- Deposits held in the SAME capacity and right (e.g., multiple savings/current/FD accounts of one individual) are aggregated.
- Deposits held in DIFFERENT capacity/right (e.g., individual vs. partner of a firm vs. guardian of a minor vs. director of a company vs. joint account) are insured SEPARATELY up to ₹5 lakh each.
- Joint accounts: if names appear in the same order across multiple joint accounts, those are aggregated (same capacity). If the order of names differs, or the group of joint holders differs, each such combination is insured separately up to ₹5 lakh.
- Deposits in different banks are insured separately — the ₹5 lakh limit applies per bank.
- If two banks holding a depositor's funds close on the same day, the funds are still insured separately per bank.

## Principal + interest
DICGC insures principal and interest up to a maximum of ₹5 lakh combined. Example: principal ₹4,95,000 + accrued interest ₹4,000 = ₹4,99,000 insured. If principal alone is ₹5 lakh, accrued interest above that is not insured (because the total exceeds the cap, not because interest itself is excluded).

## Set-off of dues
Banks have the right to set off their dues from the depositor's deposits as on the cut-off date. Deposit insurance is available only after such set-off/netting.

## Who pays the premium
The deposit insurance premium is borne entirely by the insured bank (not the depositor).

## When DICGC is liable to pay
- **Liquidation:** DICGC pays the liquidator the claim amount (up to ₹5 lakh per depositor) within two months of receiving the claim list. The liquidator then disburses to depositors.
- **Reconstruction/amalgamation/merger:** DICGC pays the difference between the full deposit amount (or the insurance limit, whichever is less) and the amount received under the reconstruction/amalgamation scheme, within two months of receiving the claim list from the transferee bank.
- **All-Inclusive Directions (AID) by RBI restricting withdrawals:** Since September 1, 2021 (Section 18A of the DICGC Act, 1961), DICGC must settle claims within 90 days total, subject to the bank submitting the depositor list within 45 days of AID imposition. Depositors must approach the CEO/administrator of the bank with a signed willingness form and attested Officially Valid Documents (OVDs) to establish identity.

## Can a bank opt out?
No. Deposit insurance is compulsory; no bank can withdraw from it. DICGC can cancel a bank's registration for non-payment of premium for three consecutive periods, or if the bank's licence is cancelled, it is wound up, it stops being a banking/co-operative company, transfers all deposit liabilities, or is amalgamated under a scheme barring fresh deposits. Deposits remain covered up to the date of cancellation.

## Disclaimer
This is non-technical, general guidance from DICGC and is not a legal interpretation of the deposit insurance scheme.
